## What is an Accessible Digital Textbook?

An Accessible Digital Textbook — or **ADT** — is a structured, interactive version of a book designed to work for all learners, including those with visual, auditory, cognitive, or motor disabilities.

An ADT is not a PDF made slightly more readable. It is a different kind of object: structured content that can be navigated by section, listened to through narration, read in multiple languages, explored through a glossary, and used by assistive technologies in the way they were designed to work.

A well-produced ADT can include:

- Structured text, organized by heading, section, and paragraph
- Audio narration in one or more languages
- Image descriptions that explain what an image shows
- Comprehension quizzes and interactive activities
- A glossary of key terms
- A table of contents for navigation
- Sign language video integrated alongside the text
- Translations into other languages
- Easy-read adaptations for learners who need simpler language

Not every ADT needs all of these. What makes it an ADT is that the content is structured, accessible, and not locked inside a static file.

Once an ADT is produced and validated, it can be preloaded on tablets or other devices and used in low-bandwidth or offline environments — which matters significantly in the contexts where it is most needed.

## What is ADT Studio?

ADT Studio is the open-source desktop tool that turns a PDF into an ADT.

It runs on your machine. The source PDF stays with you. Internet connectivity is only required when downloading updates and during the conversion process itself — the AI calls that extract and enrich content. Once an ADT is produced and validated, it works offline.

You do not need to write code to use it. The process is guided, step by step, from importing a PDF to exporting a finished package. ADT Studio is LLM-agnostic, meaning you choose which AI provider to connect — it is not tied to a single service.

ADT Studio is free to download and use. However, processing a PDF requires an AI provider, and most AI providers charge per use based on the amount of content processed. Understanding this before you start is important — see [API Keys and costs](/docs/api-keys) for a full explanation.

It is licensed under [AGPL-3.0](https://github.com/unicef/adt-studio/blob/develop/LICENSE) and developed as an open-source project by UNICEF, with support from OpenAI. The source code is available on [GitHub](https://github.com/unicef/adt-studio). It can be used by anyone: educators, publishers, parents, learners, or anyone wishing to adapt a PDF into accessible content.

## Where ADT Studio has been used

ADT Studio is part of UNICEF's broader [Accessible Textbooks for All](https://www.accessibletextbooksforall.org) initiative, which has been piloted across multiple countries in Latin America and the Caribbean, Africa, Europe, and South and South-East Asia, supporting ministries of education in converting textbooks into accessible digital formats.

Pilots with documented evidence include Kenya, Uganda, Rwanda, the Dominican Republic, Colombia, Jamaica, Nicaragua, Paraguay, and Uruguay. UNICEF Innocenti has conducted implementation research across several of these contexts.

Three live examples give a sense of what the tool produces:

- **Momo and the Leopards** — a multilingual storybook from Bhutan, produced with no manual edits to the AI output. [Open demo](https://unicef.github.io/adt-momo-storybook/)
- **Queremos Participar** — an informative reader from Uruguay, lightly edited after AI processing. [Open demo](https://unicef.github.io/adt-queremos-participar/)
- **Cuaderno 5, Chapter 1** — a grade 5 textbook with activities from Uruguay, with more extensive human curation. [Open demo](https://unicef.github.io/ADT-cuaderno5-chapter1/)

These represent a range: from pure AI output to closely reviewed content. The tool supports both approaches.

## The principles behind ADT Studio

ADT Studio is built around five ideas that shape how it works.

**Everything in one place** *(Book-level storage).* Everything related to a book lives in a single folder — the source PDF, all settings, all generated content, all versions. You can copy it, move it, or share it with a colleague without losing anything. There is no account to log into and no data stored elsewhere.

**Your work is never lost** *(Entity-level versioning).* ADT Studio never overwrites earlier results. When a step produces new content, the previous version is kept alongside it. If you preferred what you had before, you can go back to it.

**No repeated work** *(LLM-level caching).* If you rerun a step without changing anything, ADT Studio uses the saved result from last time — instantly. You only call the AI when something actually changes, which keeps processing fast and API costs down.

**You can see exactly what happened at every step** *(Maximum transparency).* Every instruction sent to the AI and every response it returned is stored in your project folder, including the cost. If a result looks wrong, you can open it, read what was sent, change it, and try again.

**Built to last** *(Minimal dependencies).* ADT Studio is deliberately kept simple — no unnecessary complexity, no bloat. This makes it easier to maintain, easier to update, and easier to use as it grows. When you install ADT Studio, you have everything you need to get started.

**Accessibility is the purpose, not an afterthought** *(Accessibility by design).* Output is checked against international accessibility standards (WCAG) before export. Every feature in the tool exists to make content work better for more learners.

## ADT Studio and Universal Design for Learning

ADT Studio puts into practice the principles of **Universal Design for Learning (UDL)** — a framework for creating content that works for the widest possible range of learners without requiring modification after the fact.

UDL asks educators and content producers to think about three things from the start: how content is represented (what learners encounter), how learners can act and express themselves (what they can do with it), and how learners are engaged (why it matters to them).

An ADT addresses the first of these directly: it offers multiple ways to access the same content — reading, listening, looking at image descriptions, switching language. ADT Studio makes it practical to produce that kind of content at scale, without requiring a team of specialists for each book.

It is also worth being honest about the current limit: ADTs produced today are primarily digitally accessible versions of print books. Activities and exercises tend to reflect what was already in the source material, made digitally accessible rather than fully adapted. Expanding what ADTs can offer — generating new, pedagogically grounded activities tailored to different learning needs — is an active area of development for the initiative.
