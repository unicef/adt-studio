ADT Studio can process most PDFs — but not all PDFs convert equally well. The quality of the output depends significantly on the quality and structure of the source file. This page helps you understand what works, what doesn't, and why.

| |
|---|
| **Starting out?** Use a short, simple PDF for your first conversion — ideally 10 to 30 pages, with clear headings and straightforward text. This lets you see the full pipeline without long processing times, and makes it easier to understand what the tool does at each step before you work with more complex material. |

---

## What makes a good source PDF

### The PDF has a real text layer

This is the most important factor. A PDF with a real text layer is one where you can click and select text directly in a PDF viewer. The text exists as actual characters in the file — not as an image of text.

When this is the case, ADT Studio can extract content reliably for conversion. The output will reflect the actual words in your document.

**✅ What to check:** Open your PDF and try to select a sentence with your mouse. If you can highlight individual words, the text layer is there. If your cursor selects the whole page like an image, or nothing at all, the PDF likely doesn't have a usable text layer.

### The document has a clear, consistent structure

ADT Studio uses AI to identify headings, sections, paragraphs, figures, and activities. This works best when the source document follows a predictable pattern — the same style for chapter titles, consistent heading levels, clear visual separation between sections.

A document where headings look different on every page, or where the hierarchy is unclear, will require more manual correction after extraction.

{/* IMAGE: simplified example of text with predictable structure VS non predictable */}

### The layout is predictable

Single-column or two-column layouts are very common, therefore better handled. Documents with complex or unusual layouts — such as magazine-style grids, overlapping text boxes, or content that relies on precise spatial positioning — are harder for the tool to interpret correctly.

## What to be careful about

### Scanned documents and image-based PDFs

A scanned PDF is a photograph of a page. There is no text layer — the document is a sequence of images. ADT Studio will not be able to extract readable text from it in the same way it can from a proper text-layer PDF.

### Complex tables

Tables that span multiple pages, or with merged cells may be more difficult to extract accurately. ADT Studio will attempt to process them, but the output may need significant manual review.

Simple tables — a few columns, clear headers, consistent rows — generally extract well.

### Headers, footers, and page numbers

Headers and footers that repeat at the top or bottom of each page are often picked up repeatedly during extraction. ADT Studio attempts to filter these out automatically, but they may appear in the extracted content and need to be removed manually during the sectioning step.

### Heavily designed or branded documents

Reports and publications with rich graphic design — full-page background images, text placed over photos, decorative elements that overlap content areas — are harder to extract reliably. The visual complexity may interfere with the tool's ability to identify what is content and what is decoration.

## Quick reference

| | Characteristic | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ | Selectable text layer | Reliable extraction |
| ✅ | Clear, consistent heading structure | Reliable sectioning |
| ✅ | Single or two-column layout | Strong output |
| ✅ | Simple tables (clear headers, consistent rows) | Extracts well |
| ⚠️ | Complex multi-page or merged-cell tables | Will need manual review |
| ⚠️ | Repeated headers, footers, page numbers | May appear in content, remove during sectioning |
| ⚠️ | Heavy graphic design or branded layout | May need manual correction |
| 🚫 | Scanned or image-only PDF (no text layer) | Text cannot be extracted |
