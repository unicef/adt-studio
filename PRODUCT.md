# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Ministry, NGO, and UNICEF-partner decision-makers evaluating whether ADT Studio can produce accessible digital textbooks (ADTs) for their education programs. Secondary audiences: the educators and content teams who would operate Studio day-to-day, and open-source contributors evaluating the project's health.

## Product Purpose

The landing page (apps/site) exists to get evaluators to trust ADT Studio and download it. Success: a decision-maker understands in one scroll what ADT Studio produces, sees the features working (demos and real output, not adjectives), and believes the project is credible enough to adopt in an institutional setting.

## Brand Personality

Credible, capable, humane. Professional polish that reads institutional-grade, carried by real product evidence. Warm mission framing (every book, every learner) without NGO-brochure softness.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS template: purple gradients, identical icon+heading+text card grids, hero-metric clichés.
- Corporate NGO brochure: stock photography, beige, lifeless.
- Dark-neon / crypto-landing flash.
- Childish edu-app aesthetics: rainbow palettes, cartoonish mascots.

## Design Principles

1. **Show, don't claim** — every feature gets demonstrated (video, live preview, real output), never just described.
2. **Practice what you preach** — a page about accessibility must itself be exemplary: WCAG 2.1 AA, keyboard-first, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected in every animation.
3. **Evidence over adjectives** — real book output, real supporter logos, real release cadence as the trust signals.
4. **Motion with intent** — animation directs attention to product proof; never decoration for its own sake.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA minimum. Reduced-motion variants for every animation. Keyboard and screen-reader parity for all interactive showcases (video player, feature switcher). The page itself is a credibility test for the product.
